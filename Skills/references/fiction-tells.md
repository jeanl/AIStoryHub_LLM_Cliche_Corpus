# Fiction-specific tells

Generic AI-writing guides are built from blog posts, marketing copy, and social media; none of them cover narrative prose. This file is the fiction-specific layer: apply it alongside `words-and-phrases.md` and `sentence-and-structure.md` whenever the content is a story, chapter, or any other narrative writing. Skip it for non-fiction registers.

Tiers match the rest of the skill: **red** = flag on sight, **orange** = flag in clusters, **yellow** = flag on repeat only.

## Body-language clichés

The AI default for "show, don't tell" is a small, recycled set of physical tells that substitute for an actual emotional beat.

- **red**: `voice barely above a whisper`
- **orange**: `a smile playing on her lips`, `let out a breath she didn't know she was holding`, `her breath caught in her throat`, `a shiver ran down her spine`, `heart hammered against her ribs`, `heart pounding in her chest`, `brow furrowed in concentration`, `ministrations`, `his eyes never leaving hers`, `unshed tears`, `eyes wide with wonder`, `steeling herself`, `with bated breath`, `piercing blue eyes`, `sagely`, `quizzically`
- **yellow**: `eyes widened in surprise`, `heart skipped a beat`, `knuckles whitened`, `took a deep breath`, `taken aback`

Fix: give the character a specific, situational physical response instead of the generic one. What does *this* body do under *this* pressure, not what every AI-drafted body does under any pressure.

## Atmosphere and sensory clichés

- **red**: `dust motes danced in the sunlight`, `the sun dipped below the horizon`, `painting the sky in hues of`
- **orange**: `the air was thick with`, `casting long shadows`, `a figure emerged from the shadows`, `casting a warm glow`, `ethereal glow`, `the words hung in the air`, `palpable`, `intricately carved`, `unbeknownst to`
- **yellow**: `dimly lit room`, `smell of ozone`, `the room fell silent`, `spread like wildfire`, `wafted`, `cobblestone streets`, `the door creaked open`

Fix: anchor description to something specific to this scene's setting and this character's point of view, not a stock environment.

## Emotional-beat clichés

- **red**: `a flicker of something akin to`, `renewed sense of purpose`
- **orange**: `couldn't shake the feeling`, `a growing sense of unease`, `couldn't help but`, `maybe, just maybe`, `mind racing with possibilities`
- **yellow**: `for the first time in a long time`, `felt like an eternity`, `something shifted between them`

## Dialogue-tag crutches

- **red**: therapy-speak reassurance in dialogue (`"I understand how you feel"`, characters defaulting to a therapist's script)
- **orange**: `said gently`, `said quietly`, sympathy-check dialogue crutch (`"Are you okay?"` used as a reflexive check-in rather than because the scene calls for it)

Fix: let the dialogue itself carry the tone. If every line needs an adverbial tag to land, the line isn't doing its job.

## Structural tells

- **red**: chapter-end resolve formula (`"...ready to face whatever lay ahead"`), `"The road ahead would not be easy"`
- **orange**: `"One thing was certain:"` scene-capper, `"an unspoken understanding passed between them"`, `"said more than words ever could"`, `"words left unsaid between them"`
- **yellow**: `"Little did she know"`

Fix: end scenes and chapters on something specific to the plot or character, not a stock gesture toward "the journey continues."

## Cliché names

Individual names are weak evidence alone; real people share them, and a name alone never proves AI authorship. They matter as a tell in **clusters** (several of these together in one cast), in a context where you already suspect AI generation, or when they're a distinctive fantasy coinage that's implausible as independent invention (`Elara`, `Aldric Wayfarer`). Treat this list as a naming-diversity check, not an accusation.

**Favorite first names (female)**: red: `Elara`. orange: `Amara`, `Lila`, `Ayla`, `Anika`, `Aria`, `Eleanor`, `Kira`, `Lyra`, `Maeve`, `Mara`, `Seraphina`, `Zara`. yellow: `Meera`, `Charlotte`, `Clara`, `Elena`, `Evelyn`, `Isabella`, `Lena`, `Maya`, `Mira`, `Sarah`.

**Favorite first names (male)**: orange: `Aldric`, `Aric`, `Cassian`, `Erasmus`, `Kael`, `Kai`, `Marcus`, `Rowan`, `Thaddeus`, `Theron`. yellow: `Samir`, `Alexei`, `Julian`, `Mateo`, `Sebastian`.

**Favorite surnames**: red: `Voss`. orange: `Abernathy`, `Blackwood`, `Castellano`, `Chen`, `Kincaid`, `Kowalski`, `Marchetti`, `Nightshade`, `Sterling`, `Thorne`, `Vance`, `Vasquez`, `Vex`. yellow: `Hargreaves`, `Hartley`, `Hayes`, `Nguyen`, `Patel`, `Reed`, `Rodriguez`.

**Fantasy & sci-fi names**: red: `Eadric`, `Eldoria`, `Aldric Wayfarer`. orange: `Freydis`, `Caldris`, `Kael Voss`, `Meridian`, `Sarenya`, `Stormweaver`, `Valdrin`, `Vantross`, `Wandermere`, `Wayfarer`.

**Full-name personas** (first+last combinations that recur suspiciously often in AI-generated fiction and examples): red: `Marcus Chen`, `Elara Voss`, `Sarah Chen`, `Elena Vasquez`, `Kira Voss`. orange: `Eleanor Vance`, `Elena Rodriguez`, `Elena Voss`, `Mara Chen`, `Michael Chen`.

**Fiction place names**: red: `Willow Creek`, `Greenhaven`. orange: `Maplewood`. yellow: `Bayville`.

Fix, if a cluster shows up in one cast: diversify surname origins and syllable patterns rather than reaching for the same handful of default combinations. `[Common First Name] + Chen/Voss/Vance` is the single most recognizable AI-fiction tell in this list. Pull from a real regional name source, a baby-name index by decade and region, or a genealogical record for the setting's period, rather than freehand generation.
