# Sentence and structure

Word-level fixes (see `words-and-phrases.md`) rarely fix AI-sounding prose on their own. Structure is the stronger signal: detectors that classify AI text (Pangram, trained on tens of millions of documents) weight structural regularity above vocabulary. Consistent sentence construction, uniform pacing, and symmetrical phrasing survive a vocabulary pass untouched. Fix the shape, not just the words.

Tiers are the same as `words-and-phrases.md`: **red** = flag on sight, **orange** = flag in clusters, **yellow** = flag on repeat / density only.

## Whole-piece shape (check this first, before any sentence-level pass)

- **The AI "sentence DNA" arc**: opening claim, expansion, contrast pivot, then a rhetorical-question or "the takeaway?" resolution, repeated at the paragraph level throughout a piece. Example: *"AI is reshaping work. It automates the routine. But it's not about replacement, it's about leverage. The takeaway? Adapt now."* One instance is a stylistic choice; the same four-beat arc repeating across every paragraph is the AI tell.
- **Motivational cadence**: claim, expansion, imperative payoff, the LinkedIn-post shape. *"Most people wait for permission. You don't need it. The choice is yours."*
- **The paragraph-reshuffle test**: can you swap two body paragraphs without the piece breaking? If order doesn't matter, you've written a list of points dressed as an argument. Fix by making each paragraph depend on the one before it, or by admitting it's a list and formatting it as one.
- **The treadmill test**: for each paragraph, name the one fact, claim, or turn it adds. If a paragraph restates the premise in fresh words without advancing it, cut it. AI prose frequently generates motion without covering distance.
- **Vocabulary range**: on pieces over ~200 words, a very narrow working vocabulary (the same 15-20 words doing all the work) reads flat. Not proof by itself (narrow technical topics compress vocabulary too), but worth a second look on general prose that should have range.
- **Symmetric development**: every point explored to exactly the same depth, every section exactly the same length. Real writing is uneven; the argument that matters most gets three times the space of a minor aside.

## Sentence-construction patterns

- **red**: voice-emotion dialogue tag ("her voice laced/tinged/dripping with X"), sunset scene-setting formula (sun hung low + casting long shadows), `"No X. No Y. No Z."` construction, choppy fragment pairs (`"All the X. None of the Y."`), contrastive negation (`"It's not just X, it's Y"`, including the split-sentence form across two sentences, and the multi-negation countdown before the reveal), false ranges (`"From X to Y"` pairing unrelated extremes for false breadth), imperative-then-payoff (`"Stop doing X. Start doing Y."`), negative parallelism (`"not only X but also Y"`), staccato one-word sentences (`"Simple. Effective. Easy."`)
- **orange**: `"A young woman named X"` story opener, emotion-cocktail construction (`"a mixture of X and Y"`), time-lapse escalation (`"days turned into weeks"`), `"Enter [X]."` dramatic introduction, `"X. Y. Z."` three-fragment payoff line, anaphora (same opening word repeated sentence after sentence), aphorism stacking (abstract declaratives in a row, "Clarity is speed. Less is more."), bold-hook opener plus short punchy lines (the social-post shape), colon reveal (`"The result: X."`), complex prepositional padding (`"taking into account," "in the context of"` stacked together), copulative avoidance (`"serves as," "stands as," "represents"` instead of plain "is"/"has"), hedge-then-assert (`"While there's no single answer, one thing is clear..."`), paradox line (`"The more you X, the less you Y"`), participial padding (a trailing `-ing` clause that restates the sentence's point instead of adding one, as in `"Revenue doubled, underscoring the company's momentum"`), resolved/over-perfect analogies (a metaphor that fits too neatly, with no seam), rule-of-three / tricolon compulsion (`"fast, reliable, and scalable"`), two-sentence negation (`"X. However, Y."`), low burstiness (every sentence lands 15-20 words, same subject-verb rhythm)
- **yellow**: parenthetical hedging aside (`"(and, increasingly, X)"`, `"(though to be fair, Z)"`), sentence-initial And/But for manufactured momentum, `"[Term] refers to"` definitional lead

## Hook and opener formulas

- **red**: community frame (`"Whether you're a founder, a marketer, or..."`, false breadth that really means "everyone"), reveal setup (`"Here's the thing..."`), temporal-landscape opener (`"In today's landscape..."`), world-state opener (`"In a world where..."`)
- **orange**: formulaic essay opener (`"in the rapidly evolving landscape of X"`, `"has emerged as a key X"`), candor opener (`"Let's be honest..."`), confession opener (`"I used to think X. I was wrong."`), contrarian opener (`"Most people believe X. They're wrong."`), curiosity hook (`"Have you ever wondered..."`), direct-imperative opener (`"Stop doing X."`), empathy opener (`"If you've ever struggled with..."`), statistic opener (`"Studies show that X% of..."` with no named source), urgency frame (`"The window for X is closing."`), `"Here's the kicker"`, `"What if I told you..."`, `"It's no secret that"`, `"Here's what you need to know"`, `"Here's the truth:"`
- **yellow**: bare rhetorical-question opener, `"buckle up"`

## Contrast and reveal scaffolds

- **orange**: `"That's where X comes in"` pivot, `"Not all X are created equal"`, `"The good news?"` reveal, `"[Problem]? Meet [solution]."` intro, `"is more than just"` elevation

## Rhetorical and structural moves

- **red**: challenges-and-prospects pivot (`"Despite these challenges, the opportunities ahead are immense"`, names nothing specific on either side), compulsive hedging / false balance (lists both sides, never picks one), editorializing markers (`"it is worth noting," "as we will see"`), sentence-level significance inflation (`"This represents a fundamental shift..."`), sycophantic flattery (`"Great question!", "You're absolutely right!"`), throat-clearing openers (`"Let me be clear," "Before we dive in"`), vague authority attribution (`"experts argue," "studies suggest"` with no name), hallucinated references (a plausible-sounding source that doesn't exist; verify anything that reads like a citation)
- **orange**: agreement mirroring (flips position to match whatever the reader just said), assumed consensus (`"As we all know"`), both-sides non-answer, clean summary close (every paragraph neatly bows itself off), definition-first opening (`"X, at its core, is..."`), disclaimer stacking, empathy preface (`"I understand this can be frustrating"`), faux-Socratic mid-essay question (`"But what does this really mean?"`), generic unfalsifiable evidence (`"a leading financial-services company"`, no name), manufactured stakes/urgency, frictionless thesis (the argument arrives fully formed, never doubting or revising itself), numbered key-takeaways wrap, over-signposting (`"First... Second... Finally..."`), reassurance close (`"You've got this!"`), restating the prompt as the opening line, symmetric/uniform development, silver-lining reflex (every negative pivots to a positive, as in "But the setback became an unexpected opportunity")
- **yellow**: elegant variation (renaming the same referent to avoid repetition, "the company... the firm... the organization"), relentless second person (`"You might be wondering. You'll find. Your journey starts here."`), tacked-on call to action, sudden register shift mid-document

## Formatting tells

Mechanical detectors for several of these already exist as regexes in `lib/corpus/structural-patterns.ts` (em-dash rate, title-case headers, UTM params, citation-markup leaks, placeholders, formulaic openers, hedge stacks, social CTA closers, parenthetical hedging). Reuse those instead of re-deriving them by eye if you're working in this repo.

- **red**: leftover AI citation artifacts (`oaicite`, `contentReference`, `turn0search`), `utm_source=chatgpt.com` in a cited URL, `attributableIndex` artifact, unfilled template placeholders (`[Company Name]`, `[Date]`, `[insert X]`), raw Markdown syntax pasted into non-Markdown text (`**bold**` or `##` showing up literally in a plain-text field), bolded-term bullet where the bold just repeats the point (`"- **Scalability:** The system is highly scalable"`), emoji used as structural bullets (🚀 / 💡 / ✅ as list markers)
- **orange**: `"In conclusion"`-style closing header, bold-as-textbook (reflexive bolding of vocabulary inside running prose), em-dash overuse (above roughly one per 1,000 words), over-structured everything (a header per paragraph in short text), uniform paragraph length, boilerplate section headings (`"Future Outlook," "Challenges and Legacy"`), curly quotes in plain-text contexts (code comments, commit messages; weak alone, since Word/Docs/iOS auto-curl, so it's meaningful mainly where nothing else would curl them), invalid or mismatched DOI/ISBN in citations
- **yellow**: American-English spelling defaulted onto UK-audience copy, horizontal-rule dividers between every section, perfectly parallel lists (every bullet the same grammatical shape and length, none of them checkable), reflexive Oxford comma, title case in every section heading, title-case template headers with `"In this article, we'll explore..."` framing, skipped heading levels (H1 straight to H3), unnecessary comparison tables for what's really three sentences of plain facts

## Register note

A single dense paragraph with no line breaks is correct in formal long-form registers (blog intros, docs, a deliberately tight email). It's a tell only in conversational registers (issue/PR comments, chat, DMs) where a person breaks a reply at thought boundaries and an LLM defaults to one unbroken block. Judge this one by register, not as a blanket rule.
